Here is a critique of the AI tools' performance for this assignment:

Overall, the AI models (Claude Opus 5 and Gemini Flash-Lite) demonstrated significant capabilities in generating initial drafts, providing structured templates, and summarizing complex information quickly. For example, Gemini generated 8 comprehensive test cases in a matter of seconds, correctly recalling specific technical details like CVE numbers and the exact date of the CrowdStrike outage. Claude effectively helped format the Markdown reports and structure the job market analysis.

However, both tools exhibited critical weaknesses that required extensive human intervention. The most prominent issue was AI hallucination. Gemini consistently fabricated minor details that sounded plausible but were factually incorrect upon verification. For instance, it invented the name of the law firm in the Mata v. Avianca case and fabricated the duration of the Ticketmaster outage. This highlights the danger of relying on LLMs as factual oracles. 

Furthermore, the AI lacked common sense regarding safety and practical testing constraints. In the physical product testing task, Gemini proposed a "Locked Rotor Test" that involved blocking the fan blades for 30-60 minutes while powered on, without any safety warnings, which could cause a fire hazard.

Even when used for verification, the AI showed confirmation bias. Claude sometimes failed to detect Gemini's errors unless explicitly prompted to be highly skeptical, often agreeing with Gemini's flawed output initially.

In conclusion, while AI is an excellent assistant for brainstorming and formatting, it cannot replace human judgment, critical thinking, and rigorous fact-checking against primary sources. The tools are useful for drafting, but every output must be treated with skepticism.
